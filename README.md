# Anup Sharma, MD, PhD

I am a board-certified psychiatrist, physician-scientist, and founder of Keystone Neuroinformatics, with doctoral training in neuroscience. My work connects clinical questions with data science, machine learning, and biomedical research, with a focus on psychiatry and applications across medicine.

My aim is to develop and evaluate computational approaches that deepen our understanding of illness, support biomarker discovery, and inform more individualized clinical interventions.

## Areas of work

- **Clinical data, biomarkers, and prediction:** Exploring clinical and research data to identify signals relevant to symptoms, disease course, and treatment response, and to develop and evaluate predictive models.
- **AI and language models for medicine:** Investigating model adaptation and fine-tuning for psychiatric and biomedical applications, with attention to clinical relevance, interpretability, and generalization.
- **Clinical AI evaluation:** Developing approaches to assess the quality, safety, and reliability of AI outputs and workflows in clinically meaningful settings.
- **Behavioral health evidence:** Developing tools that connect clinical questions, study design, outcome measures, and reproducible analysis to clarify what evidence supports and where uncertainty remains.
- **Research agents and evidence synthesis:** Building coordinated workflows for literature retrieval, appraisal, synthesis, and research planning, with source traceability, critical review, and human oversight.

## Research direction

I am particularly interested in connecting these methods to questions of individual differences, treatment selection, and response to clinical interventions. The work shared here includes exploratory analyses, research prototypes, and tools under development. Technical performance is a starting point; clinical usefulness requires careful evaluation in the intended setting.

## Collaboration

I welcome collaboration across psychiatry, neuroscience, clinical informatics, and machine learning, especially where methodological development can support clinically grounded research.
